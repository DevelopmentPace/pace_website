Our Story

Founded in 1989 by parents who refused to wait

PACE was founded by a group of concerned parents, community leaders, and educators to meet the critical need for education and care for children and adults with autism. PACE started with two group homes and 14 students — today PACE is one of the Bay Area's leading autism agencies, serving the full lifespan from preschool through adult residential care.

Understanding Autism

What we believe about autism

What is PACE's view of autism?

PACE believes autism is a challenge of interrelatedness, not a cognitive disability. Individuals with ASD are characterized by impairments in social interaction and relatedness, and often struggle with conventional language, communication, and regulating sensory input.

What does PACE's educational model focus on?

At the heart of the PACE model is helping every student develop the ability to self-regulate, to relate, and to communicate — building age-appropriate occupational, language, academic, and vocational skills that last a lifetime.

What methodologies does PACE use?

PACE blends TEACCH, SCERTS, DIR/Floortime, PECS, and ABA — selecting and combining methodologies based on each individual's unique needs.

Advocacy

PACE partners with regional and national organizations to advance autism awareness, education, and resources for families across the Bay Area.

Agency Leadership

PACE is led by an Executive Director, Assistant Executive Director, and program directors across School, Residential, Pathways, and Development — supported by a volunteer Board of Directors.

Financials & Accountability

Trusted stewardship of every dollar

While PACE receives fees for service from the California Department of Education and the Department of Developmental Services, donations allow PACE to fulfill its mission of exceptional care across every program.

Frequently Asked

Programme FAQs

How do I begin a school placement at Pruneridge or San Aleso?

Pruneridge School and San Aleso School operate as extensions of local school districts, so enrollment is by referral only. Talk with your district's special education coordinator about an alternative placement to begin the process.

Does Pathways ABA accept my insurance?

Pathways ABA lists Anthem, Aetna, Blue Shield of California, Optum, and United Behavioral Health for this site. Network participation can change, so families should confirm benefits with Pathways ABA Intake. The Pathways ABA team also coordinates services with Regional Centers and school districts, offers private pay, and can provide billing documentation for out-of-network submission.

How are residential placements arranged?

All PACE group home placements begin with a referral from the San Andreas Regional Center (SARC).

What ages does Pathways early childhood serve?

Pathways early childhood serves children ages 2.5 to 5.9 years, with class options of 9am–12pm or, for ages 4–5.9, an extended 9am–2:15pm day.

Is Porchlight really free?

Yes — Porchlight's 175 videos across 3 modules are completely free for any parent or caregiver, anywhere, with no login or fee required.

Pathways FAQ

Start with the question that matters to your family

Which Pathways service should I contact first?

If you are primarily looking for an inclusive preschool placement, use the Preschool Inquiry. If you are seeking ABA therapy, use the ABA Inquiry Form. If you are unsure, contact either team and PACE can help direct you to the appropriate starting point.

Who can receive ABA services through Pathways?

The ABA service serves children with autism and related developmental disabilities who would benefit from individualized ABA therapy. An autism diagnosis is required for insurance coverage.

What makes the ABA approach different?

Pathways combines evidence-based ABA therapy with the multidisciplinary expertise of PACE. Collaboration is at the heart of the approach. Caregivers, schools, Regional Centers, and other professionals can work together to provide consistency across the child's life. Low staff turnover, high levels of clinical supervision, and required caregiver participation support continuity and quality.

What insurance plans are listed for ABA services?

This site lists Anthem, Aetna, Blue Shield of California, Optum, and United Behavioral Health. Network participation and plan requirements can change, so families should confirm current benefits and authorization requirements with the Pathways ABA Intake Team.

Does Pathways coordinate with Regional Centers?

The ABA team coordinates services with Regional Centers when appropriate. Funding and authorization depend on the individual service arrangement, so families should confirm their pathway with the intake team and their Regional Center service coordinator.

What ages does the preschool service support?

The early childhood program serves children ages 2½ to 5.9 years in a highly supported inclusive preschool environment.

General Information

General Information

Who PACE is, who it serves, and how to get oriented.

10 answers
What is PACE?

Pacific Autism Center for Education (PACE) is a nonprofit organization serving individuals impacted by autism and related developmental disabilities through education, behavioral services, residential support, caregiver resources, and community programs.

Who does PACE serve?

PACE supports children, students, adults, families, and caregivers across multiple programs. Eligibility and referral pathways differ by service, so the best starting point depends on the person's age and needs.

What programs does PACE offer?

Current program pathways on this site include Pathways, Pruneridge School, San Aleso School, Residential Services, and Porchlight Autism Education.

Is PACE a nonprofit organization?

Yes. PACE is a nonprofit organization, and philanthropy helps support services, programs, facilities, and resources beyond public or service-based funding.

Where is PACE located?

PACE operates programs in Santa Clara County, including school campuses in Santa Clara and Sunnyvale and Pathways services in San Jose. Residential homes are located throughout the South Bay.

How do I know which PACE program is right for my child or family member?

Start with age, setting, and the kind of support needed. Pathways focuses on early childhood and ABA, Pruneridge and San Aleso are school programs, and Residential Services supports individuals who need a licensed group-home setting. The Contact page can help route you.

Does PACE provide services for adults?

Yes. San Aleso includes post-secondary-age students, and PACE Residential Services supports eligible residents into adulthood.

Does PACE diagnose autism?

PACE's website focuses on providing education and services rather than presenting PACE as a diagnostic clinic. Families who need a diagnosis for insurance or service eligibility should contact an appropriate licensed diagnostic provider.

How long has PACE been serving the autism community?

PACE was founded in 1989. The About page includes a visual timeline of major milestones, including the founding of Faces Behavioral Services in 1995 and later program growth.

What makes PACE different?

PACE brings education, clinical expertise, family collaboration, residential support, and caregiver education together within one organization, with an emphasis on individualized support across different stages of life.

Pathways

Pathways

Early childhood, ABA, intake, insurance, and caregiver participation.

11 answers
What is Pathways?

Pathways is PACE's unified brand for early-childhood inclusive preschool services and individualized ABA services, bringing those supports together in one coordinated destination.

Who can receive ABA services through Pathways?

The ABA service supports children with autism and related developmental disabilities who may benefit from individualized ABA therapy. An autism diagnosis is required for insurance coverage.

How do I begin the ABA enrollment process?

Use the ABA Inquiry Form on the Pathways page or contact the ABA Intake Team at intake@facesforkids.org or 408-785-0102. The Pathways page links directly to the inquiry form.

How do I begin the preschool enrollment process?

Use the Preschool Inquiry link on the Pathways page or contact the preschool team directly. The page keeps preschool and ABA contact options side by side so families can choose the right starting point.

What makes Pathways' ABA approach different?

Pathways combines evidence-based ABA with PACE's multidisciplinary expertise. Collaboration with caregivers, schools, Regional Centers, and other professionals is central to creating consistent support across a child's life.

How are caregivers involved in ABA?

Caregiver participation is built into the service model. Families collaborate with the clinical team so strategies can be understood, practiced, and generalized beyond direct therapy sessions.

Does Pathways coordinate with Regional Centers?

The ABA team coordinates with Regional Centers when appropriate. Funding and authorization depend on the individual arrangement, so families should confirm details with intake and their Regional Center service coordinator.

Which insurance plans are listed for ABA services?

This site currently lists Anthem, Aetna, Blue Shield of California, Optum, and United Behavioral Health. Network participation and authorization requirements can change, so benefits should always be confirmed with the Pathways ABA Intake Team.

What ages does the early-childhood program serve?

The preschool service is designed for young children in an inclusive early-childhood setting. The Pathways page lists the current program age range and enrollment details.

Can a child receive both preschool and ABA support through Pathways?

Pathways is designed to make those two service areas easier to understand together. Whether a child can receive both depends on eligibility, authorization, enrollment, scheduling, and the child's individual plan; the Pathways team can help families explore the available combination.

Where is Pathways located?

Pathways services are based at 897 Broadleaf Lane in San Jose, California. The Pathways page includes separate preschool and ABA contact information.

Pruneridge School

Pruneridge School

Elementary school placement, IEP supports, and campus questions.

10 answers
What is Pruneridge School?

Pruneridge is PACE's Santa Clara school campus serving the elementary portion of PACE's nonpublic special-education program.

Who can attend Pruneridge?

Placement is intended for students whose educational needs align with PACE's specialized program and are determined through the student's educational team and referral process.

How do I enroll my child at Pruneridge?

School placement is generally coordinated through the student's school district and IEP process rather than a direct private enrollment pathway. Families can contact PACE or their district to discuss referral steps.

Can parents enroll directly?

PACE School services are presented as nonpublic-school placements coordinated with school districts. Families should speak with their school district and PACE about the appropriate referral process.

Does Pruneridge provide speech and occupational therapy?

PACE's school model is multidisciplinary and includes speech-language, occupational, behavioral, and educational supports based on student needs and IEP goals.

Does every student have an IEP?

Students placed through the nonpublic-school process have individualized educational planning through their IEP team.

What communication methods are supported?

PACE supports individualized communication approaches, which may include speech, AAC, visuals, picture-based systems, and other strategies selected for the student.

Does Pruneridge teach life skills?

Yes. Functional independence, communication, self-regulation, social participation, and daily living skills can be integrated alongside academic instruction according to student needs.

How does PACE support challenging behavior at school?

The school uses individualized, team-based supports and draws from evidence-based educational and behavioral approaches. Goals and strategies are coordinated through the student's educational plan.

How can I schedule a tour?

Contact PACE through the Contact page or school campus contact information. Tour availability may depend on referral status and current school procedures.

San Aleso School

San Aleso School

High school, post-secondary, transition, and vocational programming.

10 answers
What is San Aleso School?

San Aleso is PACE's Sunnyvale campus for high-school and post-secondary-age students within PACE's nonpublic special-education program.

Who can attend San Aleso?

The campus serves students whose needs align with PACE's specialized high-school and transition-focused programming, through the school district and IEP referral process.

What ages does San Aleso serve?

San Aleso is designed for high-school and post-secondary students. Exact grade and age placement is determined through the educational referral and IEP process.

Does San Aleso teach vocational skills?

Yes. The campus emphasizes transition, functional academics, vocational preparation, community participation, and increased independence as appropriate for each student's goals.

What does post-secondary programming focus on?

Programming can emphasize functional communication, community access, employment readiness, self-advocacy, daily living skills, and other transition outcomes identified by the student's IEP team.

Does San Aleso provide speech and occupational therapy?

PACE's school model includes multidisciplinary educational and related-service supports based on individual student needs and IEP goals.

How are educational goals determined?

Goals are individualized through the IEP process with input from the educational team, family, district, and relevant specialists.

How does the campus support independence?

Instruction can be embedded in real-life routines, vocational activities, community experiences, self-care, communication, and decision-making skills.

Can families tour San Aleso?

Families can contact PACE and their school district about a referral and tour. Availability and timing depend on current procedures and placement considerations.

Is San Aleso a separate school from Pruneridge?

Pruneridge and San Aleso are the two PACE school campus destinations on this site. Pruneridge focuses on elementary students; San Aleso focuses on high-school and post-secondary students.

Residential Services

Residential Services

Group-home placement, supports, daily life, and Regional Center pathway.

10 answers
What is PACE Residential Services?

PACE operates licensed group homes that provide structured, individualized support for residents with autism and related developmental disabilities.

Who can live in a PACE residential home?

Placement depends on individual needs, eligibility, available openings, funding, and coordination with the appropriate Regional Center and planning team.

How many residential homes does PACE operate?

PACE describes five licensed group homes in the South Bay.

Are staff available at all times?

PACE Residential Services are structured as 24/7, 365-day supports in licensed group-home environments.

How do I begin the placement process?

Residential placement is coordinated through the relevant Regional Center pathway, including the service coordinator, individual, family or caregivers, and PACE.

Can families apply directly to a home?

Families can contact PACE for information, but formal placement typically involves the Regional Center and the individual's planning team.

What types of support do residents receive?

Supports can include daily living, communication, behavior, community participation, recreation, personal choice, health and safety routines, and individualized goals.

Do residents participate in community activities?

Community participation and meaningful everyday experiences are part of the program's emphasis on quality of life, choice, and independence.

Can families stay involved after placement?

Family and caregiver collaboration can continue as appropriate to the resident's preferences, planning process, and support needs.

How does PACE promote independence in residential settings?

Staff support residents in practicing everyday choices, routines, self-care, household participation, community access, and individualized skills rather than doing everything for them.

Referrals & Community

Finding the right service, collaboration, events, volunteering, and giving.

10 answers
What if I'm not sure which PACE service is right for my family?

Use the Contact page or the Family FAQ search to narrow the options. PACE can help route you toward Pathways, a school campus, Residential Services, or caregiver resources.

Can someone receive more than one PACE service?

Some services may complement one another, but eligibility, funding, enrollment, scheduling, and individual planning determine what combinations are possible. Contact the relevant teams to discuss a specific situation.

Does PACE collaborate with other professionals?

Yes. Collaboration with caregivers, educators, school districts, Regional Centers, therapists, and other professionals is part of PACE's multidisciplinary approach.

How does PACE individualize services?

Goals, strategies, and supports are selected around the person's needs, communication profile, educational or treatment plan, and the setting in which services are delivered.

What communication methods are supported across PACE programs?

PACE supports individualized communication, including spoken language, AAC, visual supports, picture-based systems, gestures, and other appropriate strategies.

Why is family involvement important?

Family and caregiver participation helps teams understand priorities, maintain consistency across settings, and support skills beyond direct service hours.

How can I support PACE's mission?

Visitors can donate, volunteer, participate in events, join community initiatives, shop PACE merchandise, or explore other opportunities on the Get Involved pages.

Where can I find upcoming events?

Use the Events Calendar page for upcoming PACE events, dates, and event-specific information.

Where can teens learn about volunteering and leadership?

The Youth Leadership Committee page describes PACE's student leadership and volunteer opportunities, current application information, and examples of past projects.

How can I contact PACE?

Use the Contact page for campus and program contact information or general assistance. For ABA intake specifically, the Pathways page lists intake@facesforkids.org and 408-785-0102.

Methodology

How PACE measures progress

PACE's behavioral assessments are data-driven and updated regularly. Pathways ABA uses evidence-based developmental assessments — VB-MAPP, Vineland III, AFLS, ABLLS-R — and Functional Behavior Assessments every 6–12 months, building each plan around the individual's IEP or Individualized Treatment Plan.

TEACCH · SCERTS

Structured, multidisciplinary methodologies for communication and emotional regulation.

DIR/Floortime · PECS

Relationship-based engagement and picture-exchange communication systems.

ABA

Applied Behavior Analysis with BCBA supervision and ongoing caregiver collaboration.
