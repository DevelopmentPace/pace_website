About PACE

Our story, our mission, our promise

PACE was founded in 1989 to meet a critical need — and three and a half decades later, that founding promise still guides everything we do.

Mission

Connect. Support. Educate.

To enhance the lives of people impacted by autism through innovation, exceptional education, and compassionate care.

Vision

A world in which people of all abilities live fulfilled lives.

Our Story

From two group homes and 14 students to one of the Bay Area's leading autism agencies

PACE was founded in 1989 by a group of concerned parents, community leaders, and educators as a private, nonprofit 501(c)(3) organization. At the time, autistic students were often placed in inappropriate classroom settings, and many adults were housed in unsuitable state hospitals — PACE was born after years of trying to work within that system. With a small team of dedicated educators and specialists, PACE started with two residential group homes and 14 students.

As the prevalence of autism grew, so did the demand for services. PACE expanded from education and residential care into early childhood programs, specialized behavioral support, and a free online resource for caregivers, Porchlight Autism Education. Today, PACE proudly serves the Bay Area as one of its leading autism agencies, offering comprehensive education across two school campuses, residential services, early childhood programs, and specialized behavioral support.

Understanding Autism

What we believe about autism

What is PACE's view of autism?

PACE believes autism is a challenge of interrelatedness, not a cognitive disability. Individuals with ASD are characterized by impairments in social interaction and relatedness, and often struggle with conventional language, communication, and regulating sensory input.

What does PACE's educational model focus on?

At the heart of the PACE model is helping every student develop the ability to self-regulate, to relate, and to communicate — building age-appropriate occupational, language, academic, and vocational skills that last a lifetime.

What methodologies does PACE use?

PACE blends TEACCH, SCERTS, DIR/Floortime, PECS, and ABA — selecting and combining methodologies based on each individual's unique needs.

Advocacy

PACE partners with regional and national organizations to advance autism awareness, education, and resources for families across the Bay Area.

Agency Leadership

PACE is led by an Executive Director, Assistant Executive Director, and program directors across School, Residential, Faces ABA, and Development — supported by a volunteer Board of Directors.

Financials & Accountability

Trusted stewardship of every dollar

While PACE receives fees for service from the California Department of Education and the Department of Developmental Services, donations allow PACE to fulfill its mission of exceptional care across every program.

Ages 2½–5.9 · Sunny Days Preschool

An inclusive, play-based classroom

Founded in 2005, Sunny Days Preschool is a non-public, CCL-certified preschool in west San Jose supporting autistic children, neurotypical children, and those needing extra support side by side. Classes run 8–12 students with a 1:3 adult-to-child ratio, led by certified Early Childhood Special Education teachers.

Begin Intake Process →
Ages 6–22 · PACE School

WASC-accredited, non-public special education

PACE School is certified by the California Department of Education and holds a six-year WASC accreditation earned in 2024. The Pruneridge Campus serves elementary students; the San Aleso Campus serves high school and post-secondary students — blending TEACCH, SCERTS, DIR/Floortime, PECS, and ABA around each student's IEP.

Ask your district about a referral →
Ages 18mo–13 · Faces ABA Behavioral Services

Small Steps to Big Breakthroughs

Founded in 1995, Faces ABA provides compassionate, evidence-based behavioral therapy from a San Jose clinic. Faces emphasizes assent-based, trauma-informed care, with every child supported by BCBAs and RBTs. In-network with Anthem, Aetna, Magellan, Optum, and United Behavioral Health.

Five licensed group homes across the South Bay

PACE owns and operates five group homes — Lamar, Mahalo, Matranga, Meadows, and Miracle House — each housing six residents and staffed 24/7. A licensed vendor of the Department of Developmental Services through the San Andreas Regional Center.

Frequently Asked

Programme FAQs

How do I enroll my child in PACE School?

PACE School operates as an extension of your local school district, so enrollment is by referral only. Talk with your district's special education coordinator about an alternative placement to begin the process.

Does Faces ABA accept my insurance?

Faces is currently in-network with Anthem, Aetna, Magellan, Optum, and United Behavioral Health. For out-of-network providers, Faces provides billing statements and insurance forms, and also coordinates with Regional Centers and school districts. Private pay is also available.

How are residential placements arranged?

All PACE group home placements begin with a referral from the San Andreas Regional Center (SARC).

What ages does Sunny Days Preschool serve?

Sunny Days serves children ages 2.5 to 5.9 years, with class options of 9am–12pm or, for ages 4–5.9, an extended 9am–2:15pm day.

Is Porchlight really free?

Yes — Porchlight's 175 videos across 3 modules are completely free for any parent or caregiver, anywhere, with no login or fee required.

Methodology

How PACE measures progress

PACE's behavioral assessments are data-driven and updated regularly. Faces ABA uses evidence-based developmental assessments — VB-MAPP, Vineland III, AFLS, ABLLS-R — and Functional Behavior Assessments every 6–12 months, building each plan around the individual's IEP or Individualized Treatment Plan.

TEACCH · SCERTS

Structured, multidisciplinary methodologies for communication and emotional regulation.

DIR/Floortime · PECS

Relationship-based engagement and picture-exchange communication systems.

ABA

Applied Behavior Analysis with BCBA supervision and ongoing caregiver collaboration.
